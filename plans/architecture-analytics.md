# Architecture Analytics, with Weak Spots inside it: plan

## What the person asked for
The earlier plan (plans/weak-spots.md) built a findings window: cycles, functions with too many ways through them, Used by, and a Flag that hands a finding to the agent. The person now wants something broader, and this plan replaces that one where they differ. Everything in weak-spots.md that still holds is referred to, not repeated: "as weak-spots.md Approach N".

- **One dashboard, "Architecture Analytics", opened from View**, with **Weak Spots** as its first section: the few findings worth asking about, with the same Flag → agent → fix loop.
- **"10 core dimensions", of which eight were named:** modularity, coupling, cohesion, complexity, maintainability, runtime performance, reliability, scalability. This plan says so. Decision 9 offers two more (testability and change) as sections of their own, and recommends keeping eight sections with those facts inside them; the plan is written for eight, and option (a) is a split of two sections, not a different plan.
- **For the whole system and for each block:** numbers, distributions, and the blocks and dependencies that produce each number, so a reader can go from "the system" to "this block" to "because of these three connections".
- **Objective formulas and relative comparison** (a block's rank, share and place between min, median and max within its own system), never an invented weighting or a made-up threshold. Colour only for validated thresholds and meaningful comparison.
- **A measurement is never shown as a problem; a thing not measured is never shown as healthy;** every number says, in a visible sentence, what it is computed from.
- **Used by and Reaches shown consistently**, on the exported page too where the data is there.
- The earlier plan's eight decisions revised for the broader dashboard (Decisions 1–8): the complexity limits kept provisionally but exceeding them is not a defect; the Messenger State↔UI circle kept as the demonstration; git history last.
- Afterwards, a section on the website for it (the person's own request).

Fragmentor's words for the person: PROJECT.md:124-133 (the diagram and the code), CLAUDE.md (small, simple, plain). The vocabulary is the window's own: *block*, *part*, *reaches*, *used by*, *store* (database, queue, storage), *execution*, *recording*. On screen a block is called by its `name` ("Tickets Database", "UI"); a path (`app/client/ui`) appears only where a path is meant.

## What it will do
Nothing changes on the canvas or the status bar in any phase.

- **Phase 1a (agent and terminal only; dependencies).** Nothing changes on screen.
  - `fragmentor analytics` prints, in this order: **Weak spots** (each with its block, `file:line` and evidence); then one heading per dimension, each opening with one sentence saying what is measured and from what, then the system's numbers, the top blocks with a "min · median · max" line, and the entries behind any circle or group; then **Not measured: none** or the systems and reasons. Complexity, Maintainability's size lines, Runtime, Reliability's recordings and Changes print exactly "not built yet" until their phase lands. `fragmentor analytics app/client` prints one block: its numbers, its rank among the system's blocks of its kind ("1st of 2 stores"), and the entries that make each number.
  - `fragmentor check` adds one line after Rules, only when there are weak spots: "Weak spots: 1, which is not a problem. `fragmentor analytics` lists them with their evidence; weigh them when the person asks for a review, and change nothing for them otherwise." (plural() gives "2, which are not problems").
  - On Messenger: "Parts that use each other in a circle: 1" on Client (`app/client`), citing `showMessage from src/client/ui/chatView.ts` and `dispatch from src/client/state.ts`; Database used by 6, all "on the diagram"; "Pointing at a less settled part: 1 — State (outward share 0.40) → Router (outward share 0.83)"; "UI: its 3 parts form 3 groups: Chat View · Login Form · Send Button". On Tickets: "No weak spots found", then what was looked for; Tickets Database used by 5 "on the diagram", Reminders planned; Find Event used by 2 "in the code"; "Pricing: its 2 parts form 2 groups: Price Seat · Refund For".
- **Phase 1b (agent and terminal; the code's shape).** Complexity and size fill in: "Functions with more than 10 ways through: none (most: dispatch, 7)" on Messenger; Tickets "most: bookSeats, 5"; "84 statements in 11 functions in the code, median 3, most bookSeats 12 (14%)"; "Code under no block: 4 statements (5%) in src/server.ts".
- **Phase 2 (every block's panel, and the page).** Clicking a block shows, under **Reaches**, rows for what the code reaches ("in the code: findEvent") beside the declared ones ("on the diagram: reads events"), each with Show; then **Used by** with the same two kinds of row; then **Measurements**: the block's own numbers, each followed by one visible sentence saying what it is computed from, for example "Used by 2 (in the code) · reaches 1 · outward share 0.33 (1 of 3 go out)", "Ways through its code: 1 (findEvent)", "Size: 4 statements, 5% of Tickets, 4th of 6 function blocks", "Pricing: its 2 parts form 2 groups: Price Seat · Refund For", "Held to a contract · 2 tests saved", and a line for anything not measured and why. The exported page shows the same three sections.
- **Phase 3 (the window).** **View › Architecture Analytics** opens a window, subtitle "Measured from the diagram, the code and what was recorded here. A number is a measurement, not a problem; weak spots are the few worth asking about."
  - **Weak spots** first: one heading per kind with its count. A heading is green at none and plain otherwise; a kind not measured says "not measured: <reason>", in no colour. Each row is itself **Show**, with **Flag** beside it; a flagged row reads "flagged".
  - Then one section per dimension, in this order: **Coupling, Modularity, Cohesion, Complexity, Maintainability, Runtime performance, Reliability, Scalability.** Every section has one shape: (1) the heading; (2) one body-size sentence saying what is measured and from what; (3) the system's numbers ("13 blocks · 15 dependencies · used by: min 0, median 1, max 5"); (4) rows per block, highest first, each with the number and a thin untoned bar against the system's largest, the row being Show; the first 8, then "Show all (N)"; (5) under a circle, a group or a shared table, the entries that make it, in the words Reaches uses; (6) "not measured" or "not built yet" lines, in no colour.
  - Colour appears in exactly two places: weak-spot headings at none (green), and complexity rows over 10 (grey-blue, with the words of Decision 2). Everything else is in ink, compared in words.
  - A project with several systems shows each dimension per system (systems are separate programs, PROJECT.md:210) and never sums them.
- **Phase 4 (recordings).** Where something has been recorded this session (Record on the execution bar), **Runtime performance** and **Reliability** fill in: per recording the total time, the slowest calls, the number of calls and store uses; per block its calls and the time per call; recordings that agreed with what was written down, were contradicted, or ended by throwing. A recorded step's page says "took · 3 ms". Before anything is recorded both sections say so; for drawn code they say "This code is not held to its contract, so Fragmentor cannot run it; runs of your own application would show it (plans/real-runs.md)". `fragmentor test` prints each execution's time on its head line; `fragmentor analytics` says that is where the agent gets it.
- **Phase 5 (changes, from git, last).** Under Maintainability, **Changes** fills in: commits per block in the last 12 months as counts, ranks and shares; and the weak-spot kind "changes often and is hard to follow". Until built, the line reads "not built yet", never "no changes".
- **Phase 6 (the website, and closing).** A band on the site after Commands, drawn in the site's own style, showing the window on Tickets with the words "a number is a measurement, not a problem".

## Approach
Numbered design points, each with where it lands in the code.

1. **One list of who reaches whom, shared with rules**, as weak-spots.md Approach 1. The merge inside `evaluateRules` (src/project/rules.ts:155-163) becomes an exported `reachSeen(project, reach)` returning `ReachSeen` (rules.ts:140-146) with `on: "diagram" | "code"`, `planned`, and for diagram entries `entry: string | null` (the table or channel named). It is stored in the snapshot as `reachSeen`, computed outside the `if (!atCommit)` that guards `rules` (snapshot.ts:538) and `[]` at a commit, as `rules` is. Every number that counts dependencies counts entries with `exact: true`, so rules, Used by, Reaches, coupling, cycles and cohesion cannot disagree. Approximate entries stay with the undeclared-reach warnings (reach.ts:484).
2. **One pure module, `src/project/analytics.ts`**: no node, no zod, types from snapshot.ts only (as changes.ts), so the window (app/renderer/index.html:109 loads modules unbundled), the exported page's bundle and the CLI run the same code. `holding` (reach.ts:63-69) moves here and reach.ts imports it (weak-spots.md Approach 2); a one-line `inside(a, b)`; no `fold`. It exports `analyticsOf(snapshot, extra?)`, `usersOf`, `reachesOf`, `weakSpotNote`, `isWeakSpotNote` and the limits. `extra` is `{ recordings?: Recording-like[]; changes?: Record<block, number> | null }`, supplied by the window (ui.recordings, state.ts:98) and by Phase 5; the CLI passes none and says so. It reads `snapshot.reachSeen ?? []` and `snapshot.functions ?? {}`, because an exported page made before this work has neither key (viewer.ts:43 is plain `JSON.parse`). It is computed where it is used (measurementsSection, the window on open, the CLI); no cache unless S3 says one is needed.
3. **One plain result shape, drawn by three readers.** `Analytics = { weakSpots: WeakSpot[]; dimensions: Dimension[]; notMeasured: Record<root, string[]> }`, where a `Dimension` is `{ name, system: Record<root, Measure[]>, blocks: Record<path, Measure[]> }` and a `Measure` is `{ id, label, value, unit, of: string, contributors: { path, label }[], rank?: { place, of, kind }, share?: number, band?: "warn" }`. `of` is the one plain sentence saying what the number is computed from; it is stated here and nowhere else, and each reader prints it once, visibly: the window as the body-size sentence under the measure, the inspector as one line under the number, the CLI as a line. `band` is set only where Decision 2 allows colour. The CLI prints the shape, the window draws it by looping over `analytics.dimensions`, the page draws it, and a block's own numbers are `blocks[path]`, so the three cannot disagree and nothing is recomputed in a renderer.
4. **Three words, used consistently.** A *measurement* is a number with its sentence; a *weak spot* is a finding with evidence, never a problem, with Flag; a *problem* is an error or warning (status bar, Problems, check). Weak spots never enter `diagnostics` (weak-spots.md Approach 9); check's one line is the only change to check. Findings are a view over measurements, never a second computation.
5. **Relative comparison is computed, never weighted.** For each per-block measure: rank among the system's blocks of the same kind, in two fixed phrases: by role ("1st of 6 function blocks", "1st of 2 stores") and, for parsed functions, "1st of 11 functions in the code"; share of the system's total where a total means something (statements, dependencies, calls); and the kind's min, median and max. A bar is `value / max` of that kind. A comparison is shown only with at least two things to compare; every section states its n so a small project is visibly small. No composite score exists anywhere.
6. **Colour means a validated threshold, a measured kind with no findings, or nothing.** Tones are the status bar's (styles.css:13 `--signal`, :20 `--pending`, :21 `--bad`; :603-604; :795-800). Green only for a weak-spot heading at none where the kind was measured; grey-blue only on complexity rows over 10, with the limit's words beside them (Decision 2). Counts, shares, outward share, bars and "not measured" carry no tone. Functions at 10 or under are untoned, not green: green on a count would read as a grade.
7. **Lifting.** Cycles, "kept inside" and "groups" need an entry between two deep blocks read at the level of one block's parts. One `lift(entry, parent)` maps an entry to the pair of `parent`'s children the ends fall under, or null when either end is `parent` itself or outside it. Tarjan over lifted entries finds the cycles as plans/spikes/weak-spots/cycles.mts does (weak-spots.md Approach 3); each strongly connected group of two or more is one weak spot, its evidence listing every entry "in the code" or "on the diagram".
8. **Used by and Reaches have one definition: the block's own direct entries.** `usersOf(path)` = distinct `from` blocks with an exact entry whose `to` is exactly X; `reachesOf(path)` = distinct `to` blocks of X's own exact entries, excluding X's own parts (Book Seats imports its part Reserve). The panel's rows and the Coupling numbers are the same list, so the row count always equals the number (weak-spots.md Approach 6). Ca = Used by, Ce = Reaches, outward share I = Ce / (Ca + Ce), "—" at 0/0: Martin's instability named in words, no target value, no colour. "Kept inside" (point 9) is the only measure that counts a subtree; cycles lift (point 7). Store coupling combines two sources (architecture-metrics.md VERDICT a5): code-to-code from code reach; code-to-store from declared access, which bound code reaches only through `caps` (zero code edges to Tickets Database) and drawn code through imports (Messenger's `db`). Both are in `reachSeen`; a pair is counted once; every row says which. Pass-through capabilities (block.ts:31-36, Book Seats declaring `events reads` it never makes itself) are listed "on the diagram", and the sentence says a caller declares what its parts need.
9. **Modularity is "kept inside".** For a block with at least two parts: between = distinct lifted pairs whose holder is the block; in = exact entries from outside into the subtree; out = from inside to outside; share = between / (between + in + out). The root gives the system's figure. Three counts as bars, no colour.
10. **Cohesion is groups among a block's parts.** Two parts are joined when a lifted exact entry runs between them either way, or both subtrees declare access to the same store entry; groups are the connected components, always printed in one form, "<Name>: its N parts form M groups: A · B · C". This is LCOM4 (Hitz & Montazeri 1995) adapted to blocks (product-fit.md VERDICT a7); it has no validated threshold here, so no colour and never a spot: Messenger's UI gives 3 groups because it is a store-centred client (architecture-metrics.md VERDICT a7), and Infrastructure gives 2, both right.
11. **Complexity and size piggyback on the reach parse**, as weak-spots.md Approaches 4 and 5. `parseText` (typescriptReach.ts:185) counts paths and statements per function while it walks; the parse is cached (:105-106). Reach credits each function by its own owner rules (bySymbol, then the helper rule reach.ts:294-295, then `restOwner` :162-175) and stores `ProjectReach.functions: Record<blockPath | "", { paths: number[]; statements: number; named: { name; file; line; paths; statements }[] }>`, where `""` is code under no block and `named` holds the functions over 10 plus the block's largest by paths. A distribution needs counts, not names, and the page "holds no code" (PROJECT.md:204), so no listing of every function leaves the snapshot. Statements are `ts.isStatement` nodes inside the function body except blocks, empty statements, imports and type, interface, function and class declarations; a nested function's statements are its own. Which files are read is `fileKind` (typescriptReach.ts:93, via pack.ts:84), applied at reach.ts:409: `.ts`, `.tsx`, `.mts`, `.cts`. Files under no block (`nobodys`, reach.ts:402-405, skipped before a reader is chosen today) are read with the project's one reader, or by whichever reader's `fileKind(file) === "code"` when there are several; tests stay excluded. Why a system was not read is one per-system field, `SnapshotSystem.reachNotReadBecause: string | null` (set at snapshot.ts:568 from the pack having no reader, and from the catch at :403), beside `reachRead` (:124, :568; classified at snapshotFields.ts:191). Python is out of scope (see Out of scope).
12. **Maintainability is facts, not an index.** Statements per function and block, share and rank; code under no block (statements in `functions[""]` over the total); from Phase 5, changes per block and hotspots. The book's size rules (over 10% of the code, 2 standard deviations) flag `bookSeats` (12 of 84 statements) on a 13-block example, so size is a fact with rank and share and weak-spots.md's size spike S4 is dropped (Decision 4).
13. **Reliability holds what Fragmentor can say about trust.** From the snapshot: held to a contract (`runnable`, snapshot.ts:193, with `drawnBecause` :198) and saved tests (`cases` :200; check.ts:148 computes `untested` the same way). From Phase 4: what the recordings showed. Failure in use is "not measured: needs runs of your app in use, or its monitoring".
14. **Scalability, statically, is who shares a store.** The section draws each database, storage and structure block's Used by rows (point 8) grouped by store, with the sentence "blocks that share a store cannot scale apart from it"; nothing new is computed, so no binding rule is invented. Throughput and behaviour under load are "not measurable here", in those words.
15. **Runtime from the stamps the harness already sends** (architecture-metrics.md VERDICT a1). Both harnesses stamp every call, return, throw and store use with `at`, ms since the run started (harness.cjs:191, :215, :279, :282, :292, :295; harness.py `at()` :230, called at :245, :300, :310, :315; RunEvent src/run/typescript.ts:83), and record.ts:141 pushes those very objects into `heard`, so only the `HeardEvent` type (recorded.ts:24-36) hides it. Phase 4: `HeardEvent` keeps `at`; `recordedTrace` (:98) sets `took: return.at − call.at` on each `Return` it builds for a block call; `Return` (trace.ts:100) gains `took?: number`; `returnFields` (snapshotFields.ts:265) classifies it `kept` (a written trace never has one). A store use is one `store` event sent after the operation (harness.cjs:201-217; harness.py:235-246; recorded.ts:162-164 builds the read's Return from that single event), so store uses are counted, never timed. plans/real-runs.md:69 puts `took` on `HeardEvent` keyed by step id; Phase 4 amends it to this one field on `Return`, so the two features share one word. Breakpoint holds are subtracted only from the total (run.ts:230, :270), so a recording with any `paused` event sets `took` on nothing; watch.ts, which sees `paused` (:64), sets `paused: boolean` on the `Recording` it hands to `ui.recordings.set` (:271, :334) so the window can say why (Decision 11). The 2000-call cap (harness.cjs:174, :337; `Recording.truncated`, state.ts:66) makes rows say "at least". Every runtime row says "from N recordings this session, one sample each, against stand-in stores".
16. **Changes from git, Phase 5**, as weak-spots.md Phase 4a: `codeChanges(projectDir, files)` in src/project/history.ts, `gitState` (:481) first, one `git()` call (:63); through IPC for the window and in-process for the CLI; passed as `extra.changes`. Not in the snapshot, never read at a commit (snapshot.ts:387-393).
17. **The window module is app-only.** `app/renderer/analytics.ts` builds the window from `Analytics` and calls `weakSpotFlag` from notes.ts directly; it joins `APP_ONLY` (scripts/copy-app-assets.ts:37) and the guard (:53) keeps it out of the page's bundle. `openAnalyticsWindow(analytics)` goes through `openWindow` (popup.ts:37) and follows `openCommandsWindow` (notes.ts:1021) in shape. The pure module stays bundle-safe because detail.ts needs it on the page.
18. **The window's rows are the inspector's rows, in their own class.** A row is a button that is itself Show, built like `button.problem` (inspector.ts:51-66; styles.css:1089) but classed `.measure-row`, because `.problem` draws its left border and level in `--pending` (styles.css:1089-1095, :1106-1112), the tone point 6 reserves for complexity rows over 10. `.measure-row` has `border-left-color: var(--ink-faint)`; only `.warn` recolours it. A `.measure` number in the mono face and a thin `div.meter` sized by inline width: eight lines of CSS, the only new visual element. Show calls `closeWindow()` (popup.ts:24) then `revealBlock` (canvas.ts:663). Sections are dom.ts `section`/`subsection` (:35, :42), so headings are louder than body; the one sentence per section is body-size, never a faint caption. Nothing redraws on a new snapshot (weak-spots.md Rejected).
19. **Measurements per block live in detail.ts** (`measurementsSection(block, snapshot)`) beside `reachesSection` (today `reachesSection(block: SnapshotBlock)`, :182-217, which gains the snapshot as weak-spots.md Decision 6 foresaw) and the new `usedBySection(block, snapshot)`, so the inspector (inspector.ts:354), the details window (popup.ts:259), the page (viewer.ts:91) and the as-it-was window (changes.ts:317) share them; the last two return null when `reachSeen` is empty, so an old page and a past snapshot show declared Reaches as today.
20. **Flag reuses `problemFlag`** (notes.ts:368-388) generalised as weak-spots.md Approach 8; the note's first line is "Weak spot: <headline>", it ends "`fragmentor analytics <path>` shows whether it is still there", a complexity note says the limit is provisional and the agent may reply that it should stay; a plain note, never a red ERROR (notes.ts:738-739).
21. **The agent gets the same shape.** `fragmentor analytics [block-path]` prints `Analytics` as text in the window's order and words.

**Rejected:**
- Everything in weak-spots.md's Rejected list, still.
- A composite "architecture score", a radar chart, red-to-green gauges, or the Maintainability Index: no published, validated formula (the MI is a 1992 regression over HP projects in C and Pascal, needs Halstead volume, and Microsoft's own docs call its bands arbitrary). Newman's modularity Q: published, but its "expected at random" baseline cannot be explained to a first-time user and is noise on 13 nodes.
- Abstractness and distance from the main sequence: every function block is concrete (architecture-metrics.md VERDICT a6). Connascence beyond shared entries, functional cohesion: need meaning; `review` asks the agent.
- Placement findings ("reached from outside what holds it", "could sit lower", "nothing reaches it"): PROJECT.md:17's rule re-encoded as heuristics that would need a spike and a promotion path; `review` already asks the agent whether a block should move (commands.ts:49).
- A "scale together" grouping with its own binding rules (calls bind, queues do not): a weighting; who shares a store already says it.
- Mean ± standard deviation over a dozen functions; "files holding several blocks" (a swap-time refusal, PROJECT.md:144, not a measure).
- Colour on outward share, counts or bars: no validated threshold; red there would land on every database. Red on complexity rows: Decision 2.
- Two pure modules (analytics.ts and weakSpots.ts): the hard constraint says one, and findings are a view over measures.
- Computing analytics in the main process and shipping results: the page and the window need the pure module anyway.
- Measurements on the canvas, tree, status bar, under Problems, or in a toolbar button or colour mode: clutter, and on screen by default.
- A per-block "Architecture Analytics" button and a window scoped to one block: the inspector's Measurements, Used by and Reaches rows are that block's numbers and contributors already; the constraint is one window from View.
- The dashboard on the exported page: the person asked for Used by and Reaches there, and Phase 2 gives them; the window would need the Flag injected and the window module kept bundle-safe for one reader.
- `fragmentor analytics --record`: `fragmentor test` already records every execution; it gains the time on its head line (Decision 13).
- A formula hidden behind a hover `title`: no hover on touch or by keyboard.
- Rules written from measurements ("fail the check when complexity exceeds N"): a fitness function over a count would make a measurement a problem.

## The measures
"n" is the count the headline shows. "Exact entries" are `reachSeen` entries with `exact: true`. "Lifted" is Approach 7. Where a measure is unavailable, the words are from one list in analytics.ts, printed the same in the window, on the page and in the terminal: "<Language> code is not read yet", "the code could not be read", "no code yet", "nothing has been recorded in this session" (window), "not recorded by this command; `fragmentor test` records every execution and prints its time" (CLI), "not on this page", "not built yet".
